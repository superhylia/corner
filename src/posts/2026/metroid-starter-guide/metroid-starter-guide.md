Six years ago, a little game about climbing a mountain made me cry. Isolated during the pandemic at my lowest mentally, _Celeste_ understood me in a way no other piece of art has. By no exaggeration, it changed my life. When _Farewell_ released, I climbed again only for _Celeste_ to shift my life again. I always loved games as a medium, even dreamed of making my own, but on this replay, I knew it couldn't rest as a dream. A capacity for artistry I felt but never "got" until now, I committed myself to study game design from then on out. With [a certain game's death dreadfully exaggerated](https://youtu.be/iELkFQ3vl9Y?si=Cjehq3pcnTQ_ajVj), there was no better time to check out one of the medium's crown jewels in 1994’s _Super Metroid_. Safe to say, it lived up to its acclaim. There are too many words to describe _Super_ and its impact on me, but "brilliance" would be one hell of a choice.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/supermetroid.gif", "Several sequences of Super Metroid, including breaking a glass barrier, using the Speed Booster, wall jumping, and using the Stardust Shower Special Charge Beam Attack.", null, "[Juks_Fanterisen](https://tenor.com/view/samus-aran-super-metroid-metroid-snes-super-nintendo-gif-24427546)." %}

Years later and a lot more games played, _Super Metroid_ still stands as the best game I've ever played. After such a formative title, I had to experience the rest of the series and man, what a ride it's been. I've finished most of the series and eagerly await piloting Samus for years to come. With 2027’s _Metroid Ravenous_ having millions even hungrier than Samus herself, what better time to play _Metroid_ than now? Being “the _Metroid_ fan” among friends, I thought I’d give some advice for any bounty hunters across the galaxy looking for their first mission. Along the way, I also want to have a real conversation about what it means to share something you love. My recommendations will focus on 3 of the most common appeals (taste, story, and gameplay evolution) that players like you may have.

## “I just want a taste: what should I start with?”

In one sentence, **whatever game looks coolest to you**. The beautiful thing about art is that human expression can extend from personal to communal. Oftentimes, that passion is the motivation for fans to recommend, to have someone else love what they do _like_ they do. Sometimes, however, it can be overwhelming to deal with so much information and what the "right" starting game is. All worrying about the "right" thing does is prevent what truly hooked you to _Metroid_ in the first place. (Almost) any game can be a great start for the series, so see what recommendation best fits who you are.

> [!IMPORTANT] A disclaimer on emulation and how to legally acquire ROMs/.iso files.
> With your own copy, fan emulation can often be the best and easiest way to play some of the oldest *Metroid* games. However, not everyone may know how to acquire their own or that its not as difficult as they may think. If you happened to purchase the 2D and handheld entries on Virtual Console, you can extract the ROMs for [Wii](https://www.retrogameboards.com/t/the-ripping-thread-how-to-build-your-own-legit-retro-rom-library/98/8), [Nintendo 3DS](https://wiki.hacks.guide/wiki/3DS:Virtual_Console/Extraction), and [Wii U](https://wiki.hacks.guide/wiki/Wii_U:VC_Extract). Dumping the DS, 3DS, GameCube, and Wii native games like _Metroid: Samus Returns_ and _Metroid Prime Trilogy_ is relatively straightforward if you have a [3DS](https://3ds.hacks.guide/dumping-titles-and-game-cartridges.html) or [Wii/Wii U](https://wii.hacks.guide/dump-games) as well. I will suggest official releases (when available) in this article so people can support the series, but cool ROM hacks or mods are fair game for recommendation as well.

### Acclaim and foundation: Super Metroid (1994, SNES)

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/super-guide.jpg", "Samus poses with her weapon out as drawings of the games events appear in a film strip above her.", null, "[Scan by Metroid Database](https://metroiddatabase.com/game/super-metroid/#media)." %}

The most famous and influential game in the series, let alone gaming history, _Super Metroid_ needs little introduction. Often cited as one the most acclaimed and beloved games of all time, if you've heard of *Metroid*, this is assuredly the game you’ve heard about.

_Super_ trailblazes the foundation for not just future _Metroid_ games, but the nascent Metroidvania genre as a whole. An automap (thank god) that expands as you explore Planet Zebes or activate Map Stations massively helps with player guidance. The first appearance of fan-favorite upgrades like the Gravity Suit, Super Missiles, Grappling Beam, and Speed Booster add so much to Samus’ mobility. A stackable beam system retains and combines weapon properties, encouraging unique playstyles and enhances _Metroid II_'s 4 returning beams in their versatility.

The game really does it all and Zebes is still imprinted in my mind, waiting to be explored. I can't promise _Super Metroid_ will meet "best game of all time" reputation, but anyone who loves this medium should play it.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/super.png", "Samus in Super Metroid uses the Grappling Beam to cross a chasm.", "A grappling hook? In my Metroid? Maybe the galaxy is finally at peace...", "[Screenshot by Mobygamesisreanimated](https://www.mobygames.com/game/6627/super-metroid/screenshots/snes/285894/)." %}

_Super Metroid_ can be played on Super Nintendo Entertainment System/Super Famicom - Nintendo Classics if you have Nintendo Switch Online.

### Story, challenge, and value: Metroid: Zero Mission (2004, Game Boy Advance)

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/zeromission.jpg", "Samus poses while crouching as 3 frames appear behind her soaked in red. To the left is a silhouette of Zero Suit Samus and to the right are 3 Metroids. Chozo architecture borders the box art for Metroid: Zero Mission.", "One of the coolest box arts in the entire series.", "[Scan by The Video Game Art Archive](https://videogameartarchive.com/post/708641448508227584/metroid-zero-mission-a-remake-of-the-original)." %}

Following the [two-volume](https://www.metroiddatabase.com/old_site/manga/listing.php?vid=13) [prequel manga](https://www.metroiddatabase.com/old_site/manga/listing.php?vid=19), Space Pirate leader Mother Brain discover samples of the dangerous Metroid species and how to replicate them. Against a growing threat on Planet Zebes, the Galactic Federation have one option left: the greatest of all bounty hunters, Samus Aran, is now the galaxy's last hope. As a remake of _Metroid_ (1986), _Metroid: Zero Mission_ retells Samus' first adventure while updating to the gameplay standards set by _Super_ and _Fusion_.

_Zero Mission_ is designed to be easy to speedrun and welcoming for newcomers. With one exception, 8 (optional) Chozo Statues provide a map waypoint to upgrades, though the path to get them is on you to discover. Not all upgrades and items have Chozo Statues for them, either. Samus still has to uncover all missile expansions, health Energy Tanks, Charge Beam, and the powerful mandatory 3 Unknown Items on her own. On average, _Zero Mission_ is [the shortest mainline installments](https://howlongtobeat.com/user/R2D21999/lists/180/Official-Metroid-Games), though certainly longer when going for full completion, which I encourage. A series first outside Japan, an Easy Mode unlocked by default halves damage taken and softens enemy behavior. Many find _Zero Mission_ hand-holdy and while there's a kernel of truth in that, I still found _Zero Mission_ explorative and highly replayable.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/zm.png", "In Metroid: Zero Mission, Samus uses a Super Missile against a Zebesian.", null, "[Screenshot by Metroidking](https://www.metroidwiki.org/wiki/File:ZM_Super_Missile.png)." %}

After beating _Zero Mission_, a solid emulation of the original is unlocked that includes a convenient password saving feature! A feature like this allows players to better appreciate both versions and is far too rare in rereleases (looking at you, [_Samus Returns_](https://nintendosoup.com/europe-first-look-gorgeous-metroid-samus-returns-legacy-edition/)). If you do play on original hardware, _Zero Mission_ is a better value to experience both games if you don't mind unlockables.

_Metroid: Zero Mission_ is available on Game Boy Advance - Nintendo Classics if you have the Expansion Pack subscription.

### History and isolation: Metroid (1986, NES)

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/poster.jpg", "Samus appears confidently as the games Japanese logo and text promoting the game on the Famicom Disk System appear to the left of her. Afterimages of her performing a jump are beside a screenshot for the game.", null, "[Scan by kitsunebi](https://www.retromags.com/gallery/image/13852-metroid-jp-2/)." %}

While _Zero Mission_ is pretty faithful to the original's spatial design, I don't believe it's faithful to the _experience_ of _Metroid 1_. What it plays like, how it progresses, and how it encourages exploration are quite different.

The original _Metroid_ lacks any form of player guidance, including, most famously, an in-game map. If you want to keep track of where you’ve been, you must rely on drawing your own map. Thankfully, I think anyone could give it a shot and recommend doing so: how many other games ask this of you these days? The [manual](https://www.nintendo.co.jp/clv/manuals/en/pdf/CLV-P-NAAQE.pdf) kindly gives an outline for where each area is and you can always fall back on guides if needed: [this comic-style guide from Phillip Summers is really nice](https://www.handdrawngameguides.com/free-guides/metroid-presented-by-hand-drawn-game-guides). The US/EU version saves via password and there are no Recharge Stations to regain health upon death or continue. Without downwards aiming and several iconic upgrades, Samus is noticeably stiff and more vulnerable in combat than usual.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/nes.png", "Samus stands on a grey platform, attempting to acquire an Energy Tank in the original Metroid", "One other thing about *Metroid*: the pickups are often in the open rather than hidden like they are in future games.", "[Screenshot by GTramp](https://www.mobygames.com/game/7303/metroid/screenshots/nes/976944/)." %}

_Metroid_, as the progenitor of the genre, sets the core appeal of Metroidvanias as an interconnected map with ability-gated exploration. Future games capitalized on that appeal in more potent ways, but _Metroid 1_'s friction uniquely captures that sense of hostility and isolation. Even compared to _Super_, _Metroid 1_ is truly nonlinear. Once you get the Morph Ball Bombs, which upgrades you collect and which order you fight bosses is up to you. That experience in my eyes is still worth playing, nor is _Zero Mission_ a replacement for it.

I'd be remiss to not mention snarfblam's popular ROM hack [_Metroid + Saving_ (v0.3)](https://www.romhacking.net/hacks/1186/). As per its namesake, it restores the 3 save slots from the Japanese release, while also conveniently saving player health. Pausing displays a tiny scrollable map and finding both the Wave and Ice Beams combines them. While part of me wishes each of these features were togglable, _Metroid + Saving_ is overall minimalistic. If you ever find yourself replaying _Metroid_ after a vanilla first-go, I'd recommend it.

_Metroid_ is available on Nintendo Entertainment System/Family Computer - Nintendo Classics. The latter features save slots a la _Zelda 1_, but a way to save passwords like both GBA releases or even save states comes highly recommended.

### Excitement and recency: Metroid Dread (2021, Nintendo Switch)

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/dread-kraid.jpg", "Samus in Metroid Dread fights the ginormous Kraid.", "God, this game is awesome.", "[Edited screenshots by Digital Frontiers](https://www.artstation.com/artwork/oblGlW)." %}

Being the newest game in the 2D series (and now its best-selling), _Metroid Dread_ is the closest game to what _Ravenous_ will become. *Dread*'s free ~30-minute demo lets you explore the opening of Artaria, evade 2 EMMI, acquire the first 3 upgrades, and fight the Corpius boss. What better place to start than free?

Controls are buttery smooth, the visuals are incredible, the bosses are challenging, and the game genuinely evolves the series tropes through new abilities. Full completion was even quite balanced for once, something I could never say about my (still beloved) _Fusion_. Most importantly, I found its map design to (eventually) strike a good balance between player guidance and genuine exploration and sequence breaks. _Dread_ definitely has its faults ([I find Kayin captures them well](https://kayin.moe/metroid-dread)) and I think _Super_ and _Prime_ still have stronger design overall. Despite it all, _Dread_ absolutely resonated with me and (as of writing) remains my favorite game in the entire series. I'm eager to give it yet another replay and clinch sub-4 hours in Hard Mode.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/dread.jpg", "Samus in Metroid Dread runs while shooting at a flying enemy.", null, "[Official screenshot](https://www.nintendo.com/us/store/products/metroid-dread-switch/)." %}

_Metroid Dread_ is available on Nintendo Switch.

### Immersion and first-person action: Metroid Prime (2002, GameCube)

https://youtu.be/RGxO7z4lHOc?si=HEo2d7AxGj7ttlAx

_Metroid Prime_, often confused for the mainline titles, are a phenomenal set of games and it was the original _Prime_'s reveal reception that simultaneously ended an 8 year hiatus and spurred _Fusion_'s development. Recently, _Prime 4: Beyond_’s mixed reception (relative to this series’ high standards) has caused reassessment of the _Prime_ series. While all games, especially ones so revered as the _Prime_ trilogy, eventually get their lumps, please don’t be mistaken: they're still phenomenal.

While _Prime_ lost 2D _Metroid_'s liberating mobility in the transition to 3D, I otherwise find the games to nail the appeal of the franchise. Despite being first-person shooters, combat admittedly isn't their strongest suit. Rather, they excel with incredible immersion, detailed world design, varied puzzles and platforming, and an enthralling isolation that very few games capture. 3D Metroidvanias are sadly rare, but amongst the few like my beloved _Batman: Arkham Asylum_ (2009) and _Pseudoregalia_ (2023), _Metroid Prime_ and _2: Echoes_ still carry the crown.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/prime-xray.jpeg", "Samus aims a Missile at an enemy in Metroid Prime while utilizing the X-Ray Visor, turning the game monochromatic and hazy.", "_Prime_ features visors that change how Samus percieves the world, with the X-Ray Visor being one of my favorites.", "[High-res screenshots by GameAndMic](https://imgur.com/a/metroid-prime-remastered-screenshot-collection-HgsRvhO)." %}

_Metroid Prime_ was my second _Metroid_ game and still has my favorite soundtrack and atmosphere of the entire series. Tallon IV is incredibly memorable, diverse in phenomena, and almost always does a great balancing act of player discovery and (optional) guidance. 2023's _Metroid Prime Remastered_ is refreshingly smart on adjustments while managing system-defining remade visuals and a variety of control schemes. I find pointer and motion controls significantly strengthen the movement, precision, and immersion of _Metroid Prime_ and its sequels. 2009's _Metroid Prime Trilogy_ brought the Wii Remotes pointer to _Prime 1_ and _2: Echoes_ and rightfully remains lauded in doing so. Thankfully, _Remastered_ carries this elevation forward via gyro controls and the awesome fan mod _PrimeHack_ offers similar control flexibility for those emulating _Prime Trilogy_. If witnessing the enhancement motion/gyro controls can make sounds intriguing, I reckon there are few games better suited to introduce them.

_Metroid Prime Remastered_ is available for Nintendo Switch. The original GameCube version and _Metroid Prime Trilogy_ are currently unavailable officially, though if you do find a copy, _PrimeHack_ comes highly recommended.

## “I want to be ready for Ravenous: what do I play to catch up?”

https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vY7CgzISBJk

"Catch up" implies lineage, which for most means the narrative of the games. Despite just a subtitle, _Ravenous_ is the 6th mainline 2D entry as numbered by every game’s opening. You might want to play _Metroid_ to understand why Samus is so hungry in _Ravenous_, why Ridley won’t die, and why it’s even called _Metroid_ to begin with. In that case, the **chronological order of the numbered games is as follows**:

1. _Metroid_ (1986, NES) and _Metroid: Zero Mission_ (2004, Game Boy Advance)
    1. Given _Zero Mission_ canonized it and its short length, I'd recommend reading the [prequel manga](https://metroid.retropixel.net/features/comics/metroidmanga/) beforehand.
2. _Metroid II: Return of Samus_ (1991, Game Boy), _Metroid: Samus Returns_ (2017, Nintendo 3DS), and _Another Metroid 2 Remake_ (2016, PC)
    1. Few fan projects are enshrined in a series' legacy like _Another Metroid 2 Remake_. _AM2R_ serves as _Metroid II: Return of Samus_' counterpart to _Zero Mission_. While obviously non-canon as a fan work, I find its interpretation of the events of _Metroid II_ (and stellar gameplay) compelling enough to be worth recommending alongside the original and _Samus Returns_.
3. _Super Metroid_ (1994, SNES)
    1. _Metroid: Other M_ (2010, Wii) takes place in between _Super_ and _Fusion_ as “Metroid 3.5”.
4. _Metroid Fusion_ (2002, Game Boy Advance)
5. _Metroid Dread_ (2021, Nintendo Switch)
6. _Metroid Ravenous_ (2027, Nintendo Switch 2)

You may notice that the _Metroid Prime_ sub-series is missing despite being "mainline" games. All but _Prime 4: Beyond_ take place in-between _Metroid_ and _II: Return of Samus_, for reasons the latter game explains. While certainly worth playing, _Prime_ focuses on its own story and won't really contribute to _Ravenous_ since it follows the numbered games’ story. The only mainline-relevant game I’ve yet to play, I unfortunately can’t comment on _Other M_'s role in the narrative. Easily _Metroid_'s most derided game ([for good reason as per Mothership](https://www.mothership.blog/any-objections-lady-on-linearity-subservience-and-feminine-agency-in-metroid-fusion-and-metroid-other-m/)), everyone else is likely to recommend skipping _Other M_ entirely. Once I do get around to it, I'll update this, but I can't say I've missed anything so far.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/ravenous.png", "Samus' eye peers from within her green-tinted visor.", "Eat or be eaten? I am scrumptious. :eyes:", "[Screenshot from the *Ravenous* trailer](https://youtu.be/ku-vSTDNsT8?si=sttS5PHmbYVqkyUI)." %}

In terms of plot relevance, _Metroid Ravenous_ follows the arc of Samus battling her humanity established by _Fusion_ and expanded in _Dread_. Admittedly, future games do a pretty good job of recapping prior story events: _Super_ managed to do so in just 3 minutes. As such, I wouldn't worry too much about where you start if a particular game speaks to you. My play order was pretty nontraditional (starting with _Super_, _Prime_, _Dread_, then _Zero Mission_, _AM2R_, and _Fusion_) but following the story was still straightforward. With that said, in spite and _because_ of hardware restraints, _Metroid_ usually pushes boundaries to tell engaging stories. Where else can a Game Boy game be the most consequential game in its entire series? How often do games manage to be emotionally resonant and provoking with just 24 megabits of data and no words said? Your experience, informed by order and the versions you choose to play, can drastically change your outlook on the story. For this reason, if Samus' narrative is what hooks you, I seriously implore you to try the original _Metroid II_ alongside its remakes.

## "I want to see how the series evolved: how do I get into _Metroid_?"

When people generally talk about the order of things, "lineage" as I'll describe it, usually its in the context of what's relevant to a story. In fictional film or literature, this makes sense: the story told is (usually) their core appeal. However, with games being an interactive medium, what "lineage" is in a series can get complicated. Order by graphical fidelity, the genre games employ, or even what's provided in a bundle or compilation can be equally arbitrary and logical.

For this section, though, I want to focus on lineage through the progression of gameplay; how did one of gaming's most influential series evolve? In that case, the short recommendation is **release order**:

1. _Metroid_ (1986, NES)
2. _Metroid II: Return of Samus_ (1991, Game Boy)
3. _Super Metroid_ (1994, SNES)
4. _Metroid Fusion_ (2002, Game Boy Advance)
5. _Metroid Prime_ (2002, GameCube) or its _Prime Trilogy_ (2009, Wii) and _Remastered_ (2023, Nintendo Switch) versions
6. _Metroid: Zero Mission_ (remake of _Metroid_, 2004, Game Boy Advance)
7. _Metroid Prime 2: (Dark) Echoes_ (2004, GameCube) or its _Prime Trilogy_ version
8. _Metroid Prime Pinball_ (spinoff, 2005, Nintendo DS)
9. _Metroid Prime Hunters_ (2006, Nintendo DS)
10. _Metroid Prime 3: Corruption_ (2007, Wii) or its _Prime Trilogy_ version
11. _Metroid: Other M_ (2010, Wii)
12. _Another Metroid II Remake_ (fan remake of _Metroid II: Return of Samus_, 2016, PC)
13. _Metroid Prime: Federation Force_ (spinoff, Nintendo 3DS, 2016)
14. _Metroid: Samus Returns_ (official remake of _Metroid II: Return of Samus_, 2017, Nintendo 3DS)
15. _Metroid Dread_ (2021, Nintendo Switch)
16. _Metroid Prime 4: Beyond_ (2025, Nintendo Switch/2)
17. _Metroid Ravenous_ (2027, Nintendo Switch 2)

As with any series, gameplay continues to evolve and substituting _Metroid 1/2_ with their remakes does mean that evolution gets lost. Despite being remakes, they are sequels on a mechanical level, introducing future games' abilities or entirely new ones the originals didn't have. _Zero Mission_ and _Samus Returns_ innovate with snappier controls, Aeion, the Melee Counter, and more only for them to disappear for several games. This jumbling of mechanics and features can be really jarring and is part of my suspicion to why some find _Super Metroid_ "weird" at first. Then again, how much of that perception is play order or one of gaming's greatest finally aging out of appeal?

## A Conversation: "What's it like sharing something you love?"

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/resetera.png", "A poll by entremet asks 'Would you recommend Metroid 1 NES to new fans?' on forum ResetEra. 10.4% (32 votes) vote yes, while 89.6% (277 votes) vote no.", "While not the greatest source, I'd read the range of responses on the thread with an open mind, which helps prove my point.", "[ResetEra.](https://www.resetera.com/threads/would-you-recommend-metroid-1-nes-to-new-fans.1650787/)." %}

I have a question: should new fans even play original _Metroid_ and _Metroid II_ at first, if at all? A few thousand words into this article, this is probably an odd question to ask, but I think now's the perfect time to do so. "Remake or original" has always been a point of contention, but with more eyes on _Metroid_ with _Ravenous_' announcement, its bubbled into full-on discourse. As curious players get hit with a barrage of "play this game", "skip that one", who are they even supposed to listen to? Those who suggest newer games after finding the originals (and now even _Super)_ to be frustrating, or those adamant about experiencing the series as released? As someone who's unsure where they'd land, this debate was really fascinating to spectate and rife with nuance. Already planning a starter guide anyway, a conversation with this addendum about the experience of being and becoming a fan felt appropriate.

First things first, its important to recognize that people probably mean well in all this. Guided by their own encounters, a fan probably want the best possible experience for someone new to _Metroid_. Some might find story to be what draws them most, others mobility, and yet more map design, and all of this can bias how someone onboards. Despite good intentions, influencing the "best possible" experience can unintentionally become sour. Any mechanical friction, any possible pain point, gets skipped over in fear that a newcomer may never make it far enough to engage with the series like they have.

{% image "/posts/2026/metroid-starter-guide/riku.gif", "Samus' eye peers from within her green-tinted visor.", "\"How about it, friend? We can hate Monstro *together*.\"", "[KHSource](https://khsource.tumblr.com/post/653562813858660352/kingdom-hearts-2002-dev-square-enix)." %}

I'm certainly guilty of this as well. Recently, I've put my friends on _Metal Gear_, _Castlevania_, and _Kingdom Hearts_, all 3 series with a profound impact on my appreciation of video games. Having to grapple with their experiences being different (sometimes counter) to mine has been admittedly emotional, both in reaction and reflection. When my friends didn't care for _Kingdom Hearts_' clunky platforming and level design, I instinctively just suggested to head to _Kingdom Hearts II_, my second favorite game ever made, instead. Off their first experience, I just knew they would not enjoy _Re: Chain of Memories_. Hell, outside of its narrative (which I suggested they watch in full), I loathe the game myself. When some found _Kingdom Hearts II_ to only be a mild improvement, others to cut their losses at the first game. I didn't really know how to deal.

In different circles, many friends adore these games just as I do: did I just waste someone else's time and money? Am I wrong for enjoying these games or just bad at recommending them? Who am I to ask others to be open to playing the first _Metroid_ or _Zelda_ if I myself suggested evading _ReCoM_, a narrative sequel, entirely? Writing this helped me realize that in sharing these games, the line between "me" and what I like was blurry, too blurry. As much as you can advise and praise, an honest shot is as much as you can expect. By decoupling what you _like_ from who you _are_, the tastes we have in such a diverse medium can only stand to be more flavorful.

As they evolve, games continue to grow and change what the sensibilities are: what was groundbreaking becomes standard and what was novel becomes quaint. Certainly, _Metroid_ (1986) and _Metroid II: Return of Samus_ (1991) happen to be among games perceived as "poorly aged". Their lack of maps, slower movement, mutually exclusive beams, and more are as implicit to their design as they are outdated by the series' own current standards established by _Super Metroid_. While it's a disappointing trend that players refuse to engage with older games, they are certainly old-fashioned and to the right person, frustrating. Unfortunately though, as I discussed with _Resident Evil_ (2002), remakes exacerbate this trend with tragic splash damage on older games' reputations. *AM2R*'s fame _Samus Returns_ canonicity, and _Zero Mission_'s acclaim are all microcosms of the ways remakes gain unfair credence over the originals, especially for a new audience.

https://youtu.be/hbiIPnxY0ak?si=yb7XCm8bq_3hOWa0

I think _Metroid 1_ and _2_ fans are understandably frustrated by this, rebuking an adversive, "don't bother" stigma on two games they genuinely enjoy as is, flaws and all. Made even worse is that stigma becoming defined not just by "aging out" of series standards, but actively being seen as equivalent, nay replaceable by other games. Even examining _Metroid 1_'s contemporaries, the obscurity of _Zelda_ and _Castlevania_'s remakes only fortifies this stigma. The truth is, _Zero Mission_, _AM2R_, _Samus Returns_, or any remake can't replace what was there before, even if in the worst cases of availability, they functionally do so. A rerelease can adapt, improve, and in rare opportunities, execute on a compromised vision: there are certainly some I enjoy and even prefer, myself. But as long as remakes are _competition_ and not _conversation_ between works, both games are ultimately done a disservice.

My understanding is that this sentiment is what longtime fans are likely trying to communicate to new players, that there's value in older games newer versions of them can't replicate. I don't think those who suggest _starting_ with future games necessarily mean disdain, even if the dynamics of reputation between original and rerelease can imply as such. Personally, I would agree that for most people I interact with, that's probably best for them. That being said, I do find it extremely disappointing when people disregard _Metroid_ and _Metroid II_ entirely, becoming footnotes within their own history.

I strongly believe recognizing that older games have aged doesn't mean you can't act on it, however. It's never too late to widen your view on games than the present and appreciating what came before is essential to that. Luckily, a series you already have interest in like _Metroid_ is a perfect opportunity! I find the first 2 _Metroid_ games are strong showcases for how a series forms, how expectations in design have changed, and what keeps older titles appealing. The original _Metroid_'s open-ended progression still remains unique with new things to discover on every run: did you know there are 2 Ice Beams? With its genre so young and Game Boy hardware so limiting, how _Return of Samus_ handles horror remains extremely charming and captivating in ways future remakes can't scratch. _Super Metroid_'s foundation looms large and _Dread_'s innovations stretch forward into _Ravenous_, so its safe to assume there'll never be another _Metroid_ like _Metroid 1_ and _2_ again. Whether that’s for better or worse is up to you, but in such a legendary series, even its earliest entries still surprise players decades later. Lucky us, no?
