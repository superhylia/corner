I don't know where the FFXII love on Reddit comes from just as I beat the game (maybe I'm just noticing it more?), but I'm here for it! Been typing this up since I saw this post, so I hope I'm not too late.

While I still have only a few RPGs under my belt, out of the 3 I've finished (VII, X, and most recently XII: The Zodiac Age) and the 2 I have decent progress in (42h in XVI and ~2h in I), I can confidently say this is my favorite and the best out of those. I'll try to stick to "5 reasons why Hylia enjoys FFXII" to keep things brief:

## 1. The Active Dimension Battle system feels like a fantastic evolution of ATB. 

{% image "/posts/2025/5-reasons-hylia-likes-ffxii/adb.jpg", "Vaan, Basch, Ashe, and Larsa attack Nightmares in Final Fantasy XII by stealing, attacking, using fire magic, and attacking, respectively. The Combat Log announces that Vaan couldn't steal from Nightmare B. The cursor points to Arcane Magicks, 'offensive magics for dealing damage', under Basch's command menu, but the function is disabled.", null, "My own screenshot." %}

As I'm sure we're all aware, XII was divisive on its release for breaking from series tradition and moving away from the traditional turn-based systems of I-III, which X evolved into Conditional Turn Battle, or the Active Time Battle system used in IV-IX. While personally, its divisive reputation has put many fans off, I feel like ADB is a true evolution of ATB into a more open-game environment that heightens the elements of prior games I enjoyed the most (party composition, forming roles for your characters, and maintaining strategy with timing) while adding so much depth. International Zodiac Job System and The Zodiac Age added Jobs to the game across unique License Boards, which is hard to wrap my head around as I can't imagine the game without them. It is a little weird to have to unlock licenses for equipment or [cutoff here: even magic, but its easy enough to earn License Points and it keeps progression consistent in one system.]

## 2. The world is extremely captivating. 

{% include "partials/gallery.njk" %}

At this game's release, it was the only game to be in a pre-existing universe, and Ivalice makes it worth it. Even for those who haven't played Tactics or Vagrant Story (i.e., myself), the world feels lived and breathed in. The magnitude of cities like Rabanastre surrounded by massive Est/Westersands or beautiful areas like Mr. Bur-Omisace and Phon Coast stick with you. The juxtaposition of the Old vs. New Archades, slums vs. city, and the clashing culture between them feels tangible, and without saying it directly, it shows the consequences that the Archadian Empire has on this land.

## 3. The side quests are well done and well worth doing. 

{% image "/posts/2025/5-reasons-hylia-likes-ffxii/montblanc.jpg", "Montblanc from Final Fantasy XII explains to Vaan: 'We call the vilest of the vile marks elite marks, kupo. My dream is to see each and every last elite mark fall before the swords of the clan!", "I love this guy.", "My own screenshot." %}

Usually a pain point for RPGs (especially FFX), XII does side quests with flying colors. Hunts are the main draw of XII's side quests in their first (and so far, best?) series appearance. Hunts allow you to have a variety of challenging fights that allow you to explore the expansive maps, give you extremely good rewards (increase in Clan Rank > items + access to new magic, a good amount of XP + LP gain, and the Petitioner's reward), and test your knowledge of the mechanics in a fun and engaging way. The other quests have a good variety and never get repetitive: there's only one or two "talk to this NPC across the entire map" quests, and they usually either expand the area's world or are core gameplay. While it is the longest game in the main series, I believe full completion, besides maybe the Hunt Club trophies (which I don't like much so far as I'm cleaning up my run), is not only very reasonable but recommended.

## 4. The story is fascinating. 

{% image "/posts/2025/5-reasons-hylia-likes-ffxii/power.jpg", "Princess Ashe from Final Fantasy XII holds the Netherite as the captions describe a voice: 'Even with power, we cannot change what has passed. What is done, is done.'", "As much as I love Tifa and Clive, Yuna and Aerith, Princess Ashe is maybe my favorite character of them all.", "My own screenshot." %}

I believe the reputation that this game has a bad narrative is disappointing. While FFXII is not as character-driven as prior or future games, combined with how interesting Ivalice as a world is, the political stage play narrative works very well, as I like to call it. One of the most famous localizations in gaming history is definitely a highlight: the voice acting and dialogue evocative of Early Modern/Shakesperean-inspired English are near perfect beyond compressed audio, even in TZA, unfortunately. There's a lot of pushback against XII's story due to Vaan as a protagonist being uninteresting and more than a few similarities to Star Wars, but I feel like that's a really surface-level way to view the story. Vaan is definitely not the protagonist in the sense that he drives the story (that would be Princess Ashe despite a certain handsome pirate's line); however, he absolutely justifies himself as the uniter of the characters from very different walks of life and a representation of the Empire's malice and its revolting opposition that make him fit well with the story. 

The narrative structure is definitely emblematic of Star Wars, and by the developer's admission, The Hidden Fortress (well-known as the Kurosawa film that significantly inspired A New Hope) was a significant influence on XII's story as well. XII doesn't feel beholden to its inspirations and still creates something new and exciting to follow, with a strong motif of autonomy felt in cutscene and gameplay alike. Every character, from Ashe to Vayne, has a person they want to become that's beholden by vengeance, a sense of justice or nobility to their kingdom, or how the people around the characters view them and people like them. I wish it was better-paced throughout the entire game rather than a few hours or so where nothing major really happens, but this isn't uncommon in RPGs, I suppose, and it's worth seeing it through.

## 5. Gambits tie ADB and this game together to make it personally the most satisfying game in the series.

{% image "/posts/2025/5-reasons-hylia-likes-ffxii/gambits.jpg", "Balthier's 12 Gambits are shown on screen, including a variety of items to heal and boost allies as well as enemy targets.", "The gears that keep this game turning for dozens of hours.", "My own screenshot." %}

Hiroyuki Ito, the creator of both ATB and ADB, likened Gambits (the "programming" AI control you've likely heard about) to how plays work in the NFL, and it is this mindset that I feel applies to this whole game. The strategy that ATB somewhat devalues for more reactionary gameplay is made up for with Gambits since you're assured your party will do what they're supposed to do in common denominator scenarios. Like a coach changing plays to route against the other team's offense, your party leader (pro tip: make sure a mage is your party leader, trust me) has to orchestrate the team to play up their strengths and compensate for their weaknesses. A common criticism of Gambtis is that the game plays itself, which I feel to be surface-level. Gambits take care of a lot of the repetitive or dead-air gameplay that arises from low-level random encounters on the way to areas while allowing your companions more strategic control during engaging battle. There are not enough Gambit slots for balanced characters to have an automatic solution for every situation, particularly for mages, meaning managing the battle with your party leader is critical to success. You can switch between 3 loadouts per character in TZA, which was extremely useful for regular/offensive/defensive/boss-specific setups to switch between quickly. And, of course, you can always play on Wait without Gambits and have a traditional turn-based experience. However, Active on the max speed with the usage of all the mechanics XII has on offer feels like nothing else.

This will probably get buried in this post, and writing it out in a lot of detail means I should probably get a review or starter guide of some sort done for this game, but I adore Final Fantasy XII and believe it's one of the best games I've yet experienced. I hope this helps!
